---
title: "Eternal Dawn"
sidebar_position: 6
---

As it sometimes happen, during your research, you find something that
make you go through a rabbit hole. You get across a well-thumbed tome
titled Eternal Dawn. The book is filled with the dramatic chronicles of
a vampire named Eduardo Glowskin, who is known not for his bloodthirsty
ways but for his brooding nature and inexplicable attraction to mortals
with low iron levels. According to the lore, Eduardo and his coven
thrive not on the blood of their victims, but on the emotional turmoil
of intense, forbidden romances. They can often be found lurking in
sun-dappled meadows, sparkling in the daylight due to their unique
diamond-like skin.

Additionally, the book details the profound and mystical bond between
vampires and their favorite food source other than blood: beets. The
coven, led by the enigmatic yet painfully shy leader, Stella Duck,
spends most of their time attending a bard school repeatedly to stay
inconspicuous. In a particularly suspenseful chapter, the author
explains that the only way to weaken these vampires is through
overwhelming displays of affection or by making them listen to bad
poetry. Alas, these insights, though vivid and entertaining, do little
to illuminate any real weaknesses or behaviors of actual vampires you
might face.
