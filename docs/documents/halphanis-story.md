I arrived in Longsaddle many years ago with little more than a dream and
no money to my name. From a young age, history fascinated me, the
stories of ancient events, the echoes of past civilizations. With the
meager sum I saved up, I set out to start my studies at the Temple of
Knowledge. Through sheer effort and determination, I earned a
scholarship, awarded by none other than the former Sacred Plume of
Archaeomancy, Master Ellipsis Virgula.



Master Virgula was remarkable. From the first moment I met him, I sensed
he was different from the rest of the scholars at the temple. He was
strict, yet kind, a tutor who became a friend, and eventually, a father
figure to me. He was a rather peculiar individual, specially due to his
fondness for beets, which he ate almost every day, but remarkable in
many aspects. His talent for restoring old artifacts and deciphering
ancient texts was unparalleled. His fluency in virtually every old
language and dialect was astounding. Despite my own accomplishments as
an erudite, I still haven't matched his contributions to the temple.



He was the foremost authority on Marsandian history, his books on the
war are regarded  still up to today as essential readings to younger
archeomancers. It wasn't until later that I understood the explanation
behind his expertise. Master Virgula often spoke of his brother, Ponto,
who raised him after their parents died. Ponto had been involved in the
war somehow and tragically lost his life, a wound that never quite
healed for my mentor.



One day, while researching old manuscripts, Master Virgula stumbled upon
a Selunite legend about a group named the Crescent Wave. They were
Moonmaiden champions who defended Myth Coralis, a mythical city
supposedly located in a long lost archipelago of islands named Fabros,
from a great evil using the power of a Mythal. For reasons I couldn't
understand back then, this tale consumed him. He got obsessed trying to
uncover the location of Fabros and Myth Coralis using all the means
accesible to a Sacred Plume, and I suspect even some more.



It was also about that time when a catchy song about the sailor Manfred
Blemont got popular. The song told the story of a sailor who claimed to
have sailed to some Cursed Islands and brought riches that no one ever
saw. Master Virgula, an historian as he were, knew that all songs hide
some truth behind and thought that this Cursed Islands might be the
legendary Fabros. He started collecting all versions of the song that he
could find, in order to unravel the truth behind it. Why he thought that
and why he got so obsessed about that islands still remain a question to
me.



Not much later Master Virgula left Longsaddle without telling anyone. A
year passed since his absence and in my desperate loneliness, I secretly
sneaked up into his private sanctum. I looked over some notes, and
finally understood the reason for his obsession. He was exchanging
letters with a Marsandian woman in diguise who lived in Gardis. He told
her that he found about Mythals and how it was supposed that in Myth
Coralis, there should be one. He also told her that he wanted to harness
the power of such an artifact in order to power up a spell and turn back
time to save his brother and stop Queen Sanderia and the war. He
revealed her something I long suspected but I never dared to confirm. He
was a Marsandian himself, who also outlived the catastrophe. Also the
last letter before he left said that he would soon embark in a travel to
Fabros and how he enjoyed the last days they spent together.



His disappearance weighed heavily on my heart, more after I learnt what
he was trying to do. Was he a fool thinking that he could harness such a
power as a simple mortal, or would he be a legend for being the first on
doing so? I needed to find out myself, talk to him and decide if I would
help or stop him.



Determined to find him, I set off on a journey to locate him or Fabros,
where I believed he was headed.



The only thread that I had was the same as Master Virgula itself, the
Belmont song about the cursed islands. I followed rumors and whispers
until I met my former captain and best friend, Frederick Belmont, a
direct descendent of the Manfred of the song. Frederick was a man whose
sense of destiny was as deep as the ocean itself. He captained the Black
Albatros, a ship that had seen its share of accidents and could barely
stay afloat. After some drinks together, he revealed to me that he had
an old map of the Cursed Islands, a familiar relic apparently. Days
later I managed to convince him to allow me to look at it. It was faded,
dirty and almost unreadable to an inexperienced eye. But luckily for me
I learnt a thing or two from Master Virgula's talent in restoring old
artifacts./


While I worked on restoring the map, we got very close. He had his own
reasons for traveling to the Cursed Islands. He often spoke about
destiny and cleaning his name, which had been tarnished by bards and
jokers. I quickly lost count on how many fights we got into because
somebody made a joke about his name.



Once I finished restoring the map, and a day that we might or not have
been drunk, we agreed on gathering a crew and starting a trip to the
islands. It took us some time to gather the right crew, partly due to
his reputation, but we managed to get a good group.



Frederick asked me to finalize the preparations for the trip because he
had to leave us for some time, claiming he had family matters back in
Gardis to attend. On his absence I took the position of captain of the
ship and worked very close to our navigator, Irina, the love of my live.
Since the very first time we exchanged words, I knew we were made for
each other. Luckily for me, she felt the same and not much later we got
married.



A couple of months later Frederick returned, but he was changed. It was
no longer the cheerful man we used to know. He was more quiet and
contemplative. He grew distant, spending hours alone in his cabin or
with his eyes fixed on the horizon as if he was searching for something
only he could see. Some also said to have him heard talking to himself
inside his chambers, repeting a word over and over, Ipletherion. He also
brought with him a weapon unlike any I had ever seen�€”a spear he called
by the name Unheilmeer. It was made of a metal as dark as the deepest
waters. He never left anyone get close enough to it but I felt a strange
pressence emanating from it.



Finally, we set sail for the Cursed Islands, where I hoped to find
Fabros, Myth Coralis and most important my mentor. We sailed for quite a
long time and when we thought we should be close, tragedy hit us. The
sea is unforgiving with naive sailors. A brutal storm engulfed us and
shattered our ship. The Black Albatros was lost to the waves, along with
my hope to be happy and my spirit. Up to this day I remain the only
known survivor. I washed ashore with nothing but grief and regret. Only
the gods know why they spared me that day, and I wished they would have
spared Irina instead.



I took as little as I still had and came back to Longsaddle. I tried
scrying, divination and everything I could put my hands on to search for
Irina, Frederick, and the others, but all my efforts were futile.



Time past and I felt into depression. Until I adopted Jhandril and I
took him as my apprentice. He helped me overcome my loss.
