## Overview

The **Order of the White Scale** is a monastic order devoted to [Kelemvor, the Judge of the Dead](/docs/pantheon/kelemvor-the-judge-of-the-damned.md).

The order is based in the **Scales Hills**, south of **West Nevington**, but its members can be encountered throughout [Norberia](/docs/regions/norberia/).

Followers of the White Scale are known to appear in places where **great catastrophes and numerous deaths are about to occur or have already occurred**, accompanying the dead on their final journey to the other side.

## Servants of the Judge of the Dead

The Order of the White Scale dedicates itself to the teachings of **Kelemvor** and the natural passage between life and death.

Its members often travel to places struck by war, disaster, or other events involving great loss of life. There, they tend to the dead and accompany those who have passed beyond the threshold on their journey to the other side.

Strangely, members of the order have sometimes been known to **arrive shortly before a catastrophe occurs**, giving them an ominous reputation among those familiar with their ways.

---

## Neutrality

The White Scale generally remains **neutral in conflicts**, seeing its duty to the dead as more important than the disputes of the living.

Its members possess divine gifts, but they do not necessarily use them to prevent every death.

Those who have already passed beyond the threshold or who have willingly accepted that their time has come are generally allowed to complete their journey rather than being pulled back from it.

---

## Enemies of the Undead

While the White Scale remains neutral in many conflicts, there is one matter on which the order does not compromise:

**Undeath.**

The undead represent a violation of the natural passage between life and death that the order exists to protect.

Members of the White Scale have therefore **sworn their lives to fighting undead creatures** wherever they are found across the lands of [Norberia](/docs/regions/norberia/).

For the followers of Kelemvor, death itself is not the enemy.

**The refusal to accept death is.**
